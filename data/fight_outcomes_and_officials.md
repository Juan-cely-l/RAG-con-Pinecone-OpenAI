# Fight Outcomes and the Role of Officials (Educational Summary)

This file is an unofficial educational summary for a course RAG project. It is not an official rulebook.

## Finishes

- Knockout (KO): a fighter is rendered unable to continue because of a legal strike.
- Technical knockout (TKO): the referee stops the fight because a fighter cannot intelligently defend themselves, or the fight is stopped by the ringside doctor (doctor stoppage) or by the fighter's corner.
- Submission: a fighter taps out (physically or verbally) or is rendered unconscious or injured by a choke or joint lock. A "technical submission" occurs when the referee stops the fight because a fighter is choked unconscious or a limb is at risk of injury without a tap.

## Decisions

When the scheduled rounds are completed, the result is determined by the judges' scorecards. Three judges score each bout.

- Unanimous decision: all three judges score the bout for the same fighter.
- Split decision: two judges score the bout for one fighter and the third judge scores it for the other fighter.
- Majority decision: two judges score the bout for the same fighter and the third judge scores it as a draw.
- Unanimous draw: all three judges score the bout as a draw.
- Majority draw: two judges score the bout as a draw.
- Split draw: one judge scores for each fighter and the third judge scores a draw.

## Technical decision

A technical decision happens when an accidental foul (for example, an accidental headbutt or eye poke) causes an injury that stops the fight after a certain point in the bout, commonly after two rounds have been completed in a three-round fight. The judges' scorecards, including the partial round, decide the winner.

## No contest

A no contest means the bout ends without a winner. Common causes are an accidental foul that stops the fight before the required number of rounds is completed, or a result that is later overturned by the athletic commission (for example, after a failed drug test).

## Disqualification

A disqualification (DQ) occurs when a fighter commits an intentional foul that prevents the opponent from continuing, or when a fighter accumulates repeated fouls after warnings and point deductions.

## The referee

- The referee is the only official inside the fighting area and has authority over the bout.
- The referee starts and stops the action, separates fighters, enforces the rules, issues warnings, and deducts points for fouls.
- The referee can stand fighters up from the ground when there is a lack of action.
- The referee's main duty is fighter safety: stop the fight when a fighter can no longer intelligently defend themselves.

## The ringside doctor

The ringside physician can examine a fighter between rounds or during a pause called by the referee. If the doctor determines that a fighter cannot safely continue, for example because of a severe cut affecting vision, the fight is stopped and recorded as a TKO (doctor stoppage), unless the injury was caused by an accidental foul.

## The judges

Judges sit outside the fighting area, score each round independently using the 10-point must system, and do not consult with each other. Their scorecards are collected after each round.
