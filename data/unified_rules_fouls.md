# Fouls Under the Unified Rules of MMA (Educational Summary)

This file is an unofficial educational summary for a course RAG project. It is not an official rulebook. Athletic commissions publish the authoritative text, and rules have been revised over time.

## Common fouls

The following actions are generally considered fouls under the Unified Rules of Mixed Martial Arts:

- Butting with the head (headbutts).
- Eye gouging of any kind.
- Biting or spitting at an opponent.
- Hair pulling.
- Fish hooking.
- Groin attacks of any kind.
- Small joint manipulation (attacking individual fingers or toes).
- Strikes to the back of the head or the spine.
- Throat strikes of any kind, including grabbing the trachea.
- Kicking the head of a grounded opponent.
- Kneeing the head of a grounded opponent.
- Stomping a grounded opponent.
- Holding the shorts or gloves of an opponent.
- Holding or grabbing the fence or ropes.
- Spiking an opponent to the canvas on the head or neck (piledriver).
- Using abusive language in the fighting area.
- Attacking an opponent during the break between rounds or while the opponent is under the care of the referee.
- Timidity, such as avoiding contact, intentionally dropping the mouthpiece, or faking an injury.
- Flagrant disregard of the referee's instructions.

## The grounded fighter

A fighter is commonly considered grounded when any part of the body other than the soles of the feet touches the fighting area. Under the 2017 revisions adopted by many commissions, a fighter with both hands (palm or fist) touching the canvas is also considered grounded. Kicks and knees to the head of a grounded fighter are fouls.

## 12-to-6 elbows

Downward "12-to-6" elbow strikes were long prohibited under the Unified Rules. Several commissions removed this prohibition in later revisions, so whether it is a foul depends on the jurisdiction and rule version in force.

## Consequences of fouls

- The referee may issue a warning for a foul.
- The referee may deduct a point, or more than one point, from the fighter who committed the foul. Point deductions are applied to the scorecards of the round where the foul occurred.
- Repeated or flagrant fouls can lead to disqualification (DQ).
- A fighter who is fouled may be given recovery time. For an accidental low blow, the fouled fighter is commonly allowed up to 5 minutes to recover.

## Accidental fouls

If an accidental foul makes a fighter unable to continue early in the fight, the bout is usually declared a no contest. If an accidental foul stops the fight later in the bout, the outcome is commonly decided by the judges' scorecards as a technical decision (see fight_outcomes_and_officials.md).

## Intentional fouls

If an intentional foul causes an injury that stops the fight, the fighter who committed the foul generally loses by disqualification.
